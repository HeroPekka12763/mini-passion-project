About Me

A little bit about me, as of late July I finally got to resume working on this project after 4 weeks of Summer Classes. The first week was tiring, but during the last 3, it was smooth sailing from there.

If I didnt already mention before, I'm a rising junior in high school with high aspirations to pursue a career in the field of computer science. I always try to balance home life with whatever passion project I always try to do next, and things are going pretty well so far. This is my first time making a website that didn't have a set end goal, but rather something relaxingly unstructured that I can still keep track of. Because of this structured yet unstructured nature of this project, I'm still able to make use of my free time to study for the upcoming SAT, as well as plan ahead for any roadblocks I might experience next school year. Besides this, I plan on learning other programming languages as well, such as Python, C++, and Javascript, Javascript ESPECIALLY because of how useful it can be for making websites.

I've always been interested in computers, I'm pretty sure like ever since I was like 6 or 7 years old (pun intended), I've always taken a liking to them. I remember when I was younger, I would always get excited whenever my elementary teachers pulled out one of those tablet or chromebook carts, cause by then I knew that I was gonna be using a computer for the rest of the day. It was always a fun experience hopping on cool math games and playing whatever niche game came up first. It's funny to look back at it too, because half of the games I played back then are no longer available, and the ones that are still around have either been outdated as hell for a while or monetized into the ground. I can only imagine the developers of those games begging their executives not to make their games P2W only to get laid off the next day.

Besides that, I remember developing an interest for coding when I was around 9-10 ish. I was casually surfing the web when I stumbled upon this website called Scratch, and it was hella fun using it. I made an account just so that I could share my own games, and I ended up making a few cool concept games that weren't really that impressive. At the time though, I knew I was onto something, and I wanted to learn more about coding after that.

I recall sitting in my room listening to my virtual 5th grade zoom class (this was during Covid), and I remember how I would always be off-track from the lesson. Rather than actively listeing and participating, I would be on Scratch messing around, or on Codecademy trying to learn Python 2 as much as I could until I hit a paywall. I would always try to be sneaky about it too, until my teacher informed my parents that I wasn't paying attention in class. My parents weren't happy in hearing that their 10 year old son wasn't paying attention in class, they were quite upset about it, in fact. I still tried to convince them that I was learning something useful, but at the time, they were really not having it. In truth though, they were still supportive of my interests in coding, and they still are.

Fast forward to 7th-8th grade, I took a computer class in middle school that covered basic coding logic on this website called code.org, similar to Scratch. The difference this time was that there was an actual curriculum based entirely upon dragging blocks in the right order, and that curriculum had cartoon themed lessons where you had to complete an objective or get a cartoon character to a certain area on a map. I remember how I would try to finish the lessons as fast as I could, even going as far as to skip ahead and finish the curriculum months in advance. By the end of the semester, I was the one being asked by my classmates for help on the lessons, and I was acting like the biggest know-it-all (I wasn't even that good anyway.)

Despite these shortcomings, I still want to pursue computer science as a career, and I want to make sure that I'm academically and mentally prepared for the challenges that come with it.
